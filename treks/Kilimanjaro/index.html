About the Expedition

The World's Highest
Freestanding Mountain

Rising abruptly from the sun-drenched East African savannah to an astonishing 5,895 meters (19,341 feet), Mount Kilimanjaro is the pinnacle of the African continent and one of the legendary Seven Summits of the world.

What makes the Machame Route Expedition with Bootpaths unparalleled is its golden acclimatization profile. Following the time-honored mountaineering philosophy of "Climb High, Sleep Low" — trekking up to the dramatic Lava Tower (4,630m) before descending to sleep at Barranco Camp (3,960m) — our climbers achieve a remarkable 96%+ summit success rate.

Over seven unforgettable days, you will journey through five distinct climatic ecosystems: starting in lush tropical montane cloud forests teeming with blue monkeys, traversing heather moorlands dotted with prehistoric Giant Senecios, conquering the famous volcanic scramble of the Great Barranco Wall, and finally pushing past Stella Point to celebrate sunrise upon the crater rim of Uhuru Peak beside ancient glaciers.

Day 1

Moshi to Machame Gate & Tropical Rainforest Ascent to Machame Camp

Moshi Base (850m) → Machame Gate (1,800m) → Machame Camp (2,835m)
11 km hike +1,035m
Park Registration, Montane Rainforest Trail & Blue Monkeys

Depart Moshi after breakfast for a scenic 45-minute drive through coffee and banana plantations to Machame Gate (1,800m). Meet your Wilderness First Responder (WFR) certified lead guides, porters, and mountain chef while park permits are finalized.

Enter the southwestern slopes into pristine montane cloud forest. The well-defined trail winds through ancient moss-draped camphorwood trees, towering ferns, and creeping vines. Look out for the black-and-white Colobus monkeys and lively Blue monkeys leaping through the canopy.

Arrive at Machame Camp (2,835m) in the late afternoon, perched where the rainforest transitions into heath moorland. Enjoy a warm washing basin, hot ginger tea with popcorn, followed by a hearty multi-course alpine dinner in our heated dining tent.

Day 3

Shira to Lava Tower (4,630m) & Descent to Barranco Valley (3,960m)

Shira Camp (3,750m) → Lava Tower (4,630m) → Barranco Camp (3,960m)
10 km trek Climb High, Sleep Low
The Acclimatization Gold Standard • Lava Tower & Giant Groundsels

This is the most critical acclimatization day of the expedition. Ascend steadily eastward across the stark, boulder-strewn alpine desert towards the imposing volcanic plug known as Lava Tower (4,630m / 15,190 ft) — nicknamed the "Shark's Tooth".

Stop at the base of Lava Tower for hot lunch and rest at high altitude. While some climbers may experience mild headache or breathlessness, spending 1-2 hours at 4,630m kickstarts vital acclimatization mechanisms.

Following lunch, descend 670 meters through the dramatic Senecio-filled Barranco Valley. Dropping down to sleep at Barranco Camp (3,960m) ensures maximum physical recovery while reaping the massive acclimatization benefits of the high climb.

Day 4

Barranco Wall Scramble to Karanga Camp (3,995m)

Barranco Camp (3,960m) → Barranco Wall Crest (4,200m) → Karanga Camp (3,995m)
5 km scramble Barranco Wall
The Famous "Breakfast Wall" Scramble & Heim Glacier Panorama

Start the morning with the most exhilarating physical feature of the trek — conquering the 257-meter Great Barranco Wall. Though it looks intimidating from camp, it is an enjoyable, non-technical Class 2 scramble requiring hands and feet for balance.

Experience the legendary "Kissing Rock" corner with our guides coaching every step. Reaching the crest of the wall (4,200m) awards you with thunderous applause and unparalleled panoramic views of Kibo's hanging glaciers.

Traverse across a series of rolling volcanic ridges before dropping into the green Karanga Valley — the last water point on the mountain — and ascending to Karanga Camp (3,995m) for a well-earned afternoon rest.

Day 5

Karanga Camp to Barafu High Basecamp (4,673m) & Summit Preparation

Karanga Camp (3,995m) → Alpine Desert Scree → Barafu High Camp (4,673m)
4 km trek +678m Basecamp
Alpine Desert Ridge, Mawenzi Peak Vistas & Pre-Summit Staging

Leave Karanga Camp following the Mweka junction trail up towards Barafu Camp. The trail climbs steadily across desolate, wind-swept alpine scree with zero vegetation — a landscape resembling the surface of Mars.

Reach Barafu Camp (4,673m / 15,331 ft) — "Barafu" meaning "Ice" in Swahili — perched dramatically on an exposed rocky ridge crest. The jagged spires of Mawenzi Peak dominate the eastern horizon, while Kibo's summit crater towers directly overhead.

Complete a thorough summit gear inspection (headlamps, extra lithium batteries, thermal balaclavas, insulated down mitts, thermal flask). Have an early carbohydrate-rich dinner at 05:30 PM and sleep by 07:00 PM in preparation for the midnight summit bid.

Day 6

Midnight Summit Push to Stella Point (5,756m) & Uhuru Peak (5,895m) – Roof of Africa

Barafu Camp (4,673m) → Stella Point (5,756m) → Uhuru Peak (5,895m) → Mweka Camp (3,100m)
17 km push 5,895m SUMMIT
The Crowning Moment • Sunrise Over Africa, Glaciers & Uhuru Peak

Wake up at 11:30 PM for warm ginger tea and biscuits. At midnight, begin the monumental summit climb by headlamp through steep volcanic scree switchbacks between the Rebmann and Ratzel glaciers.

After 5-6 hours of rhythmic, mental grit, reach Stella Point (5,756m) on the crater rim just as dawn begins to break across the horizon — casting a magnificent orange glow over the glacier fields. Rest briefly with warm tea.

Continue for another 45-60 minutes along the snow-draped crater rim to Uhuru Peak (5,895m / 19,341 ft) — the highest point on the African continent! Celebrate at the world-famous summit sign, marvel at the ancient Rebmann and Furtwängler Glaciers, and take life-defining photos. Descend back to Barafu for warm lunch and rest, before continuing down to Mweka Camp (3,100m) for a well-deserved night's sleep in rich, oxygenated air.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about climbing Mount Kilimanjaro with Bootpaths

Why is the 7-Day Machame Route recommended over shorter routes?

+

The 7-Day Machame Route boasts a phenomenal summit success rate exceeding 96% because of its ideal "Climb High, Sleep Low" acclimatization profile. On Day 3, climbers ascend to Lava Tower (4,630m) for lunch and acclimatization before dropping to sleep at Barranco Camp (3,960m). Shorter 5 or 6-day routes rush this process, dramatically increasing altitude sickness risks. 7 days provides your body the exact physiological time needed to produce red blood cells and adapt smoothly.

Do I need mountaineering or technical climbing experience?

+

No technical mountaineering skills, ropes, harnesses, or crampons are required to climb Mount Kilimanjaro on the Machame Route. It is a non-technical trekking peak. However, it is an extremely strenuous high-altitude endurance hike. Trekkers should have good cardiovascular stamina, leg endurance, and be comfortable hiking 5 to 7 hours daily with a daypack on rugged mountain trails.

How does Bootpaths manage high-altitude safety on the mountain?

+

Safety is our uncompromising priority. All Bootpaths lead guides are certified Wilderness First Responders (WFR). Every expedition carries bottled emergency medical oxygen, a portable hyperbaric Gamow bag, pulse oximeters for mandatory twice-daily blood oxygen (SpO2) and heart rate tracking, and specialized high-altitude first aid kits. Our guide-to-client ratio is 1:2 on summit night to ensure dedicated individual attention.

What is Bootpaths' policy on porter welfare (KPAP)?

+

Bootpaths strictly adheres to the ethical standards established by the Kilimanjaro Porters Assistance Project (KPAP). We guarantee fair living wages, three wholesome hot meals a day, proper wind-and-waterproof sleeping shelters, high-altitude mountain gear, and a strict 15 kg maximum load limit per porter verified at every national park weigh station.

How cold does it get at the summit of Kilimanjaro?

+

While daytime temperatures in the lower rainforest and moorland range from 18°C to 25°C, high camp at Barafu averages around -5°C at night. During the midnight summit push to Stella Point and Uhuru Peak (5,895m), temperatures regularly drop between -10°C and -18°C with wind chill. Our layered packing checklist (thermal underwear, heavy down jacket, windproof shell, thermal mitts, and balaclava) keeps you warm and protected.

What visas, flights, and vaccinations are required for Tanzania?

+

You should book flights arriving at Kilimanjaro International Airport (JRO), which is a 45-minute drive from our base hotel in Moshi. Tanzania tourist visas are available online (e-Visa) or upon arrival ($50 for most nationalities / $100 for US passport holders). A Yellow Fever vaccination certificate is mandatory if arriving from or transiting through endemic countries. We also recommend standard travel vaccinations (Tetanus, Typhoid, Hepatitis A).
