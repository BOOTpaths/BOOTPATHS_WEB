About the Trek

India's Pristine
Tiger Haven & Living Heritage

Tucked within the majestic Nelliampathy and Anamalai ranges of the Western Ghats, Parambikulam Tiger Reserve spans 391 square kilometers of exceptionally preserved tropical wilderness recognized globally as a UNESCO World Heritage biodiversity hub.

The Parambikulam Rainforest & Bamboo Raft Trek by Bootpaths offers an authentic glimpse into virgin ecosystems untouched by modern commercial tourism. Here, towering canopies of moist deciduous and evergreen forests shield Asia's most revered living giant: the legendary Kannimara Teak — an awe-inspiring natural wonder over 450 years old with a monumental girth exceeding 7 meters.

Accompanied by Kerala Forest Department certified indigenous Kadar tribal trackers, you will navigate remote jungle corridors, observe massive herds of Indian Gaur grazing in misted glades, track Asian elephants, and glide peacefully across emerald reservoir waters on traditional handcrafted bamboo rafts.

Phase 1

Anappady Base Assembly, Forest Clearance & Kannimara Teak Pilgrimage

Anappady Checkpost (520m) → Moist Deciduous Trail → Kannimara Teak Enclave (600m)
5 km Trail 600m
Morning Briefing, Kadar Naturalist Intro & Colossal Ancient Teak

Assemble at the Parambikulam Tiger Reserve entry base at Anappady by 08:00 AM. Complete permit verification with the Kerala Forest Department and meet your Bootpaths trek lead alongside licensed indigenous Kadar community naturalists.

Commence the overland trail through towering rosewood and teak plantations into virgin moist deciduous jungle. Breathe the crisp mountain air as mist lifts off the valleys, accompanied by the musical chorus of the Malabar Whistling Thrush and canopy calls of Nilgiri Langurs.

Arrive at the revered Kannimara Teak grove. Standing over 49 meters tall with a girth of 7.15 meters, this 450-year-old tree was spared during colonial logging operations due to local tribal reverence. Learn about tribal legends and the ecological heritage of the sanctuary.

Phase 2

Kariyanshola Rainforest Trail, Stream Crossings & Traditional Feast

Kannimara Grove (600m) → Kariyanshola Stream (680m) → Parambikulam Eco-Hub (550m)
5 km Trail 680m Peak
Moist Evergreen Jungle, Wildlife Spoor & Authentic Kerala Meal

Enter the pristine Kariyanshola trail, an enchanting pocket of semi-evergreen and moist tropical rainforest. Dense wild bamboo brakes and hanging lianas canopy the trail, creating a cool microclimate even at high noon.

Hop across clear granite boulders of seasonal jungle streams. Your Kadar tracker will guide you in examining fresh animal tracks along the sandy riverbanks — identifying pugmarks of leopards, claws of sloth bears, and hoof impressions of Indian Gaur.

Conclude the trek loop at the eco-development centre, where a wholesome, freshly prepared traditional Kerala lunch served in eco-friendly banana leaves awaits the team.

Phase 3

Parambikulam Reservoir Bamboo Rafting & Anakkal Vayal Vista

Rafting Jetty (540m) → Reservoir Island Channels → Anakkal Vayal Tower (610m)
3 km Raft 540m Lake
Silent Water Glide, Waterfowl Observation & Panoramic Watchtower

Don standardized, certified lifejackets and board traditional woven bamboo rafts at the reservoir jetty. Guided by expert local boatmen using long bamboo poles, glide smoothly into the calm emerald expanse of Parambikulam lake.

Drift quietly along submerged forest islands where cormorants, darters, and ospreys perch on sun-bleached snags. Watch the shoreline for wild Gaurs, spotted deer herds, and Asian elephants arriving for afternoon hydration.

Conclude with a visit to the Anakkal Vayal watchtower offering sweeping panoramic vistas across the entire sanctuary basin, followed by hot cardamom tea and certificate presentation by 05:00 PM.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about trekking and bamboo rafting in Parambikulam Tiger Reserve

Is advance booking required for the Parambikulam Tiger Reserve Trek?

+

Yes, absolutely. Because Parambikulam is an ecologically sensitive Project Tiger reserve governed strictly by the Kerala Forest and Wildlife Department, daily visitor quotas are capped to protect wildlife habitats. Bootpaths handles all official forest department clearances, tribal naturalist guide assignments, and bamboo raft slot reservations ahead of time.

How do we reach the Parambikulam base at Anappady?

+

Geographically located in Palakkad district, Kerala, road access into Parambikulam is routed via Pollachi and Topslip in Tamil Nadu. Anappady is the entry checkpost of the tiger reserve, situated roughly 40 km from Pollachi, 85 km from Coimbatore Airport/Station, and 100 km from Palakkad. Bootpaths can arrange private vehicle pick-ups from Coimbatore, Pollachi, or Kochi upon request.

What makes the Kannimara Teak so famous?

+

The Kannimara Teak (Tectona grandis) is among the largest and oldest known living teak trees in the world, with an estimated age of 450 to 500 years. It stands at an astonishing height of 49.5 meters and boasts a girth of 7.15 meters. Revered as an abode of forest deities by the native Kadar tribe, it was honored by the Government of India with the prestigious Mahavriksha Puraskar.

Is bamboo rafting safe and suitable for beginners and families?

+

Yes. The bamboo rafts used in Parambikulam reservoir are wide, buoyant, and handcrafted by local tribal craftsmen with immense structural stability. The reservoir waters are tranquil and free of swift rapids. Every guest is outfitted with ISI-certified life jackets and accompanied by trained indigenous boatmen. Children above 6 years and senior citizens can comfortably participate.

What strict conservation and plastic rules must trekkers follow?

+

Parambikulam Tiger Reserve enforces a strict plastic-free and noise-free policy. Disposable plastic water bottles, single-use bags, smoking, alcohol, and music speakers are strictly prohibited. Trekkers are checked at Anappady gate and provided access to clean drinking water refill points. Visitors must carry reusable flasks and adhere to "Leave No Trace" principles.

What wildlife might we encounter along the trails and reservoir?

+

Parambikulam boasts one of the highest densities of Indian Gaur (wild bison) in South India, frequently seen grazing peacefully on forest edges. You may also spot Asian elephants, spotted deer, Nilgiri langurs, Malabar giant squirrels, wild boars, and over 250 species of birds including the Great Indian Hornbill and Sri Lanka Frogmouth. While tigers and leopards inhabit the core zone, they remain elusive and nocturnal.
