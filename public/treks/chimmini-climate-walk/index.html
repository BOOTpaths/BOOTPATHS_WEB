About the Climate Walk

Walk the Living Lungs
of the Western Ghats

The Chimmini Climate Walk is more than an adventure — it is a transformative field journey into the crucial microclimates, cloud interception canopies, and hydrological lifeblood of Kerala's Western Ghats.

Nestled within Mukundapuram in Thrissur, the Chimmini Wildlife Sanctuary safeguards over 85 square kilometres of contiguous tropical evergreen and semi-evergreen forests, surrounding the serene Chimmini reservoir. Here, ancient Dipterocarpus trees rise over 40 metres into the sky, anchoring an ecosystem that generates rain clouds, cools regional temperatures, and replenishes the Kurumali river basin.

Guided by indigenous Malayan and Kadar tribal trackers from the local Eco-Development Committee (EDC), you'll decode natural weather indicators, listen to the acoustic rhythms of the jungle canopy, glide on silent bamboo rafts, and witness wildlife in its most undisturbed sanctuary.

Trek & Climate Highlights

Rare rainforest encounters that reconnect you with the planet's ecological pulse

01

Virgin Evergreen Canopy

Trek beneath colossal Dipterocarpus and Cullenia trees with giant buttress roots that sequester massive carbon tons and create unique microclimate zones.

02

Reservoir Bamboo Rafting

Drift silently across the calm turquoise waters of Chimmini reservoir on handcrafted bamboo rafts, framed by mist-clad peaks and reflecting rain clouds.

03

Great Indian Hornbill Sanctuary

Spot the iconic state bird of Kerala flying above the canopy. Chimmini is celebrated for Malabar Trogons, Southern Birdwings, and Nilgiri langurs.

04

Tribal Ethnobotanical Tracking

Learn ancestral plant lore and tracking techniques from indigenous forest watchers who interpret wildlife footprints, bird calls, and medicinal herbs.

05

Stream Hydro-Ecology

Follow crystal-clear streams feeding the Kurumali river, studying nature's filtration sponges, water temperatures, and riparian micro-habitats.

🌎

360° Catchment Ridge Overlook

Ascend through the upper forest tier toward the Padikkattumala ridge, beholding an uninterrupted panorama of the entire water catchment basin, rolling Western Ghats peaks, and the sprawling evergreen wilderness below.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need to Know

Common queries regarding the Chimmini Climate Walk, permits, leeches, and logistics

Unlike standard sightseeing treks, the Chimmini Climate Walk is an experiential ecological immersion. Along the trail, you learn how Western Ghats evergreen forests act as microclimate moderators, atmospheric moisture interceptors, and carbon sinks. Led by tribal guides, you observe canopy humidity variations, water catchment sponges, and the impacts of seasonal rainfall shifts on endemic wildlife.

The trek is rated Moderate. The trail covers approximately 16 km over 2 days through rainforest terrain, moderate ascents, and river fringes. Anyone with reasonable walking fitness, including families with teenagers and beginners, can comfortably complete the walk.

Yes, terrestrial leeches are natural bio-indicators of healthy tropical rainforests, particularly during and following the monsoon (June to November). BOOTpaths provides specialised leech protection socks and natural herbal deterrents (tobacco powder/salt) that prevent contact.

The assembly point is the Echippara Eco-Tourism Center, Chimmini Dam, located in Thrissur District, Kerala. It is approximately 38 km from Thrissur Railway Station, 32 km from Chalakudy, and 60 km from Cochin International Airport (COK). Taxis and buses are readily available via Varandarappilly.

Yes! BOOTpaths manages all official Kerala Forest Department clearances, sanctuary entry passes, and Eco-Development Committee (EDC) guide bookings in advance. You only need to provide government-issued photo ID upon booking.

Accommodation is provided in clean, secure Forest Eco-Lodges / Tented Camps managed in harmony with sanctuary guidelines. Clean bedding, blankets, and hygienic shared restroom facilities with running water are provided.

Absolutely! Chimmini is an internationally recognized Important Bird Area (IBA) with over 160 bird species, including Great Indian Hornbills, Malabar Grey Hornbills, Ceylon Frogmouths, and dozens of endemic Western Ghats butterfly species like the Southern Birdwing.

To preserve the delicate rainforest ecosystem and deliver an intimate learning experience, we strictly cap group sizes between 6 and 14 participants per batch.
