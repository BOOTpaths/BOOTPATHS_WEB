Trek Overview

Walk the Singalila Ridge
Beneath the Sleeping Buddha

Join BOOTpaths for six days of trekking, with an arrival-day transfer from Siliguri to Manebhanjang and a return to Siliguri on Day 6.

From forest paths and prayer flags to open ridgelines, Sandakphu–Phalut brings the Eastern Himalaya into view one walking day at a time. On clear days, the panorama includes Everest, Kangchenjunga, Lhotse, and Makalu—four of the world's five highest mountains. The Kangchenjunga group forms the celebrated Sleeping Buddha skyline, while the route passes through mountain settlements and the ancient mossy forests of Singalila National Park.

This is a rewarding, non-technical trek for people who prepare for long walks, steep climbs, sustained descents and basic mountain stays. The crowning glory is the extended 21 km ridge traverse from Sandakphu to Phalut—a world-class walking journey above a sea of clouds.

The Journey

Your Journey from the Plains to the Ridge

Day 0–Day 6 is seven calendar days and six overnight stays. Distances, elevations and walking times are planning estimates.

⏰

Quick 7-Day Route Overview (Day 0 – Day 6)

Summary of each day's travel, elevation, distance and night stop

▾

Day 0 Siliguri to Manebhanjang (Drive ~85–100 km / 4–5 hrs • Overnight Manebhanjang, ~1,930 m)

Meet at confirmed Siliguri pickup point (11 AM). Drive into Darjeeling hills, check in at base stay, document and gear verification, and evening safety & green-trail briefing.

Day 1 Manebhanjang to Tumling (~11–13 km / 6–7 hrs • Overnight Tumling, ~2,970 m)

Start early, climb towards Chitrey monastery and Meghma to Tumling. Substantial first ascent gaining roughly 1,000 m in sleeping elevation; monitor health and pacing.

Day 2 Tumling to Kalpokhri (~12–13 km / 6–7 hrs • Overnight Kalpokhri, ~3,180 m)

Possible sunrise over Kangchenjunga, route via Gairibas into Singalila National Park. Checkpoints, forest trails and the sacred black-water lake of Kalpokhri.

Day 3 Kalpokhri to Sandakphu (~6–8 km / 3–4 hrs • Overnight Sandakphu, ~3,636 m / 11,929 ft)

Walk towards Bikheybhanjang before the steep final climb to West Bengal's highest point. Settle into accommodation, rest, and evening viewpoints of the western giants.

Day 4 Sandakphu to Phalut (~21 km / 8–10+ hrs • Overnight Phalut, ~3,600 m / 11,811 ft)

The main endurance day of the programme! Long open ridge via Sabargram with repeated rises and falls. Unrivalled panoramic views of Everest, Lhotse, Makalu and Kangchenjunga.

Day 5 Phalut to Gorkhey (~14–15 km / 5–7 hrs • Overnight Gorkhey, ~2,300–2,400 m)

Early sunrise at Phalut viewpoint followed by a long descent from high ridge into silver fir, bamboo forests, and the peaceful riverside valley stay of Gorkhey.

Day 6 Gorkhey to Srikhola & Siliguri (Trek 12–16 km / 5–7 hrs • Drive 5–7 hrs to Siliguri)

Final morning walk through Samanden and Rammam to Srikhola roadhead. Vehicle transfer back to Siliguri (total 10–14+ hrs). Evening arrival; buffer hotel night recommended.

📅

Detailed Day-by-Day Activity Breakdown

Official daily trail details, highlights, elevation profiles, and guides

▾
Day 0 • Travel Day

Siliguri to Manebhanjang (Drive ~85–100 km / 4–5 hrs • ~1,930 m)

▸
Meet at the confirmed Siliguri pickup point (11 AM cutoff) and drive into the Darjeeling hills. Settle into the base stay, meet the local guide and complete the document and equipment checks. The evening briefing covers the route, weather, walking pace, health reporting and green-trail practices. Keep activity gentle and turn in early.

Travel Tip: Arrive in the region the previous evening where possible. If arriving on Day 0, use only a flight or train that reaches before the organiser's confirmed pickup cutoff. Airport and NJP pickups must be agreed explicitly.
Day 1 • Mountain Trek

Manebhanjang to Tumling (~11–13 km / 6–7 hrs • ~2,970 m)

▸
Start early and climb towards Chitrey, continuing through the Meghma area to Tumling. This is a substantial first ascent, gaining roughly 1,000 m in sleeping elevation. Walk at a steady conversational pace and use short, regular breaks. Where access and time permit, the guide can introduce the monasteries and settlement history along the way.

At Tumling, settle in and enjoy the skyline if the weather clears. Report headache, nausea, unusual tiredness or dizziness promptly; the next day's plan depends on how the group is feeling.
Day 2 • Border Ridge

Tumling to Kalpokhri (~12–13 km / 6–7 hrs • ~3,180 m)

▸
After a possible sunrise view, follow the route through the Gairibas area and climb towards Kalpokhri. Forest sections, uneven paths and undulating terrain mean that distance alone does not describe the effort. Follow the guide through park and border checkpoints.

Kalpokhri takes its name from its dark lake. Spend a quiet moment by the water, keeping away from sensitive edges and respecting local customs. Do not wash, throw offerings or discard anything into the lake. Warm up, eat well and prepare for the steeper climb to Sandakphu.
Day 3 • Bengal's Roof

Kalpokhri to Sandakphu (~6–8 km / 3–4 hrs • ~3,636 m / 11,929 ft)

▸
Walk towards Bikheybhanjang before the steeper final ascent to Sandakphu, the highest point in West Bengal. Although this is a shorter distance, the climb is sustained and the altitude is noticeable. Keep the pace easy and avoid treating the final slope as a race.

On arrival, settle into the accommodation, have a warm meal and rest. If conditions and your health permit, make a short, gentle visit to a nearby viewpoint. The mountain panorama may reveal itself in brief openings in the cloud; allow time to simply watch.

Preparation for Phalut: Refill drinking water, pack lunch and trail snacks, check headlamps, and keep wind and rain layers accessible. The guide will confirm trail conditions and group readiness before proceeding.
Day 4 • Main Endurance Day

Sandakphu to Phalut (~21 km / 8–10+ hrs • ~3,600 m / 11,811 ft)

▸
Begin early, with a brief sunrise view only if it fits the departure plan. Follow the long ridge towards Phalut, commonly passing the Sabargram area. Clear weather opens wide views towards the Everest group and Kangchenjunga. The terrain repeatedly rises and falls, so this is not a simple level walk between two similar elevations.

This is the hardest day of the programme. Wind, cold, poor visibility and limited shelter can make progress slower. Keep water and food within reach, take planned breaks and stay with the group. Do not separate to photograph distant viewpoints or follow an alternative trail. Phalut accommodation is limited and basic. Arrive, change into dry layers, eat and rest.
Day 5 • Forest Valley Descent

Phalut to Gorkhey (~14–15 km / 5–7 hrs • ~2,300–2,400 m)

▸
If conditions allow, visit the Phalut viewpoint early before beginning the long descent. The trail drops from the high country into forest and bamboo, with a very different atmosphere from the exposed ridge.

Take short, controlled steps on steep or slippery sections. Trekking poles can help with balance and reduce fatigue on the descent. Avoid racing downhill; knees and ankles still have another walking day ahead. Reach Gorkhey and settle into the village stay. Spend the afternoon resting or taking a gentle local walk with permission. Keep well away from unsafe riverbanks and do not enter fast or cold water.
Day 6 • Final Trek & Return Drive

Gorkhey to Srikhola & Siliguri (Trek 12–16 km • Drive 5–7 hrs)

▸
Leave early for the final walk, commonly through Samanden and the Rammam area towards Srikhola. The exact trail, village stops and usable vehicle pickup point must be confirmed by the local operator. Meet the transfer vehicle and continue the road journey to Siliguri.

This is a long combined trekking and driving day. Allow approximately 10–14 hours or more from departure to Siliguri, depending on the confirmed trail, breaks, road conditions and traffic. An evening or late-evening arrival is possible.

Important Logistics Advice: Plan an additional Siliguri hotel night and fly or take your onward train the following day. This extra stay is outside the six-night trek package unless separately included in your booking. Avoid a same-day flight booking based on an optimistic arrival estimate.

🛡️

A Flexible Plan is Part of a Good Trek

Mountain judgement, turnaround ethics, and safety protocols

▾
  • ● Guide Authority: The trek leader and local guide must be able to shorten, reroute or stop the trek when weather, trail access or participant health makes that necessary. Participant safety always takes precedence over completing the advertised route.
  • ● Ridge Modifications: A split night on the ridge may be sensible for some groups, but changes the six-night plan unless another section is altered. It must be agreed in advance, not assumed to be included.
  • ● Acclimatization Awareness: Fitness does not prevent altitude illness. Symptoms must be reported early. Never ascend with symptoms; worsening symptoms demand descent.
  • ● Turnaround Judgement: Treat turnaround decisions as sound mountain judgement, not failure. The mountains will always be there.

🏡

Simple Rooms & Hospitality

Expect basic homestays, trekker huts or lodges according to confirmed availability. Rooms may be twin, triple or dormitory-style with shared bathrooms and limited privacy. Do not assume attached toilets, room heating, reliable hot showers or hotel linen. Phalut is especially basic and has limited capacity.

🍲

Fresh Food with Local Character

Wholesome and filling meals: rice, dal, vegetables, roti, porridge or other locally available dishes. Noodles, soup, eggs or regional dishes where supplies allow. Lunch may be packed on longer stages. Vegetarian meals are easiest to arrange; vegan or gluten-free diets need advance confirmation.

💧

Water & Everyday Practicalities

Carry reusable bottles with ~2 litres capacity, refilled at stays. Drink to thirst without forcing excessive water. Carry small-denomination cash; do not depend on UPI or ATMs after the base village. Power banks are essential as battery life drops in cold and electricity is intermittent.

🩺

Altitude Awareness in Every Briefing

Fitness does not prevent altitude illness. Report headache with nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue immediately; never ascend with symptoms. Worsening symptoms require descent. Breathlessness at rest requires urgent medical attention. Never leave an unwell trekker alone.

Additional Items to Carry for Winter Treks

Winter along the Singalila ridge brings sub-zero temperatures, freezing wind, and potential snow or icy sections. Supplement your packing with:

Warmer Down Jacket (650+ FP)

Thick baffled insulation rated for -5°C to -10°C ridge winds at Sandakphu and Phalut.

Extra Heavy Thermals & Socks

Heavyweight merino wool top and bottom; extra thick dry socks strictly reserved for sleeping.

Insulated Waterproof Gloves

Fleece liner gloves paired with a windproof and water-resistant outer glove or mitten.

Balaclava & Traction Microspikes

Full face protection against biting gales; microspikes and gaiters when recommended for snow/ice.

Eight Weeks to Build Your Trail Confidence

Start from your current fitness and build gradually. The aim is to walk comfortably on consecutive days, manage long descents and carry your expected pack.

Week Walking and Aerobic Endurance Strength, Mobility and Practical Preparation
Week 1 4 walks of 30–40 minutes at an easy to brisk pace. 2 light sessions: chair squats, step-ups, calf raises and simple core work.
Week 2 4 walks of 40–50 minutes; one may extend to 60–75 minutes. Practise balance and gentle mobility. Start wearing your trek boots.
Week 3 3–4 walks of 45–60 minutes plus a 90-minute outing. Add stairs or a short hill once weekly. Start with a light 2–3 kg pack if comfortable.
Week 4 Maintain regular walks; extend the long outing to 2–3 hours. Strength twice weekly. Practise controlled downhill steps and trekking pole balance.
Week 5 One 3–4 hour hilly walk, plus 3 shorter walks. Move gradually towards the pack weight you will actually carry on trail.
Week 6 A 4–5 hour outing and an easy 60–90 minute walk the following day. Practise snacks, water intake, layering adjustments and foot care on the move.
Week 7 One 5–6 hour hilly outing, followed next day by 2–3 easy hours if recovered. Use your full footwear and pack setup. Resolve rubbing, blisters or equipment problems.
Week 8 Reduce total training. Take short comfortable walks and arrive rested. No last-minute exhausting sessions. Finalise gear, sleep patterns, and travel arrangements.

Leave a Lighter Footprint on Singalila

BOOTpaths encourages quiet, responsible travel, care for the protected border landscapes, and deep respect for Himalayan communities.

1. Carry Waste All the Way Out

Carry back all wrappers, tissues, peels, and sanitary waste. A lodge bin is not proof of safe waste processing; pack it all out.

2. Space for Wildlife & Red Pandas

Stay on trails. Never chase animals, pick flowers, play bird calls, or blast music. An encounter with a red panda must never become a pursuit.

3. Protect Water & Sacred Lake

Keep soaps away from streams and lakes. At Kalpokhri sacred lake, respect local religious customs; no washing or throwing offerings.

4. Respect Homes & Sacred Shrines

Ask before photographing residents or children. Remove footwear at monasteries and never step over prayer flags or sacred stones.

5. Support Local People with Dignity

Choose local meals, pay fair prices, avoid wasteful food orders. Treat guides, teahouse hosts, porters and drivers with dignity.

6. No One Left Behind Policy

Follow the lead and sweep trek leaders, regroup at junctions, and never take unscheduled shortcuts. We start as a team and finish as a team.

01. What makes Sandakphu–Phalut special?

+

The route combines a famous Himalayan panorama with an extended ridge walk, mountain villages and forest descents. Phalut adds a substantial walking challenge beyond a Sandakphu-only visit.

02. Can we see Everest from Sandakphu?

+

On clear days, Everest can be seen in the distant western mountain group with Lhotse and Makalu. Clouds and haze can hide the view completely.

03. What is the Sleeping Buddha?

+

It is the reclining-figure outline associated with Kangchenjunga and its neighbouring peaks. Your guide can help you identify the skyline when visibility permits.

04. Is this trek easy or moderate?

+

For this full itinerary, moderate is the more useful description. The first climb, approximately 21 km Phalut stage, long descent and consecutive walking days demand preparation.

05. Can a first-time Himalayan trekker join?

+

A well-prepared beginner may be suitable after discussing fitness and health with the organiser. Previous short hikes and back-to-back walking practice will help.

06. Do I need climbing skills or ropes?

+

The usual route is non-technical. Snow, ice or severe weather can still make sections unsafe and may require equipment changes, rerouting or cancellation.

07. Is there an age limit?

+

The organiser must set the participation policy. Suitability depends on health, fitness and supervision; do not describe this itinerary as suitable for every age without assessment.

08. How high do we go?

+

The highest planned point is Sandakphu, approximately 3,636 m. Accommodation elevations and published readings vary slightly; Phalut is around 3,600 m.

09. How far do we walk in total?

+

Budget approximately 80–85 km for the proposed complete walking route. The total will be revised after the vendor confirms trail choices and the final pickup point.

10. How long is the longest walking day?

+

Sandakphu to Phalut is approximately 21 km. Allow roughly 8–10 hours, sometimes longer, including breaks and depending on weather and group pace.

11. Why does the programme begin with Day 0?

+

Day 0 is the arrival and transfer day, including the Manebhanjang overnight stay. Days 0–6 total seven calendar days; it is not an extra day outside the package.

12. How many nights are included?

+

The revised plan contains six: Manebhanjang, Tumling, Kalpokhri, Sandakphu, Phalut and Gorkhey. The vendor must confirm and price all six.

13. Is Srikhola an overnight stop?

+

Not in this seven-day plan. We walk to the confirmed Srikhola roadhead and transfer to Siliguri on Day 6.

14. Can the long Phalut stage be split?

+

Potentially, if authorised accommodation and logistics allow. An extra ridge night changes the programme unless another stage is redesigned; it needs a separate agreement.

15. Are all route spellings the same places?

+

Common variants include Manebhanjang/Maneybhanjyang, Kalpokhri/Kalipokhri, Gorkhey/Ghorkey and Srikhola/Shrikola. Match bookings to the actual property and pickup location.

Travel and Access (Q16 – Q30) 16 – 30

16. Which airport should I book?

+

Bagdogra Airport, code IXB. Siliguri is the regional meeting hub, but the airport is a separate pickup location that must be confirmed.

17. Which railway station is best?

+

New Jalpaiguri, code NJP, is the main long-distance rail gateway. Check the booking carefully; it is not the same station as Siliguri Junction.

18. Can I fly from Kochi?

+

Yes, search COK–IXB connecting itineraries. Prefer one protected booking and confirm that the arrival time works with the group transfer.

19. Can I fly directly from Bengaluru?

+

Non-stop BLR–IXB options may be available for your dates. Verify the actual flight before booking; route pages do not guarantee daily non-stop operation.

20. Which trains should I consider from Ernakulam?

+

Check Vivek Express and Aronai Express services to NJP. Verify the current service number, ERN or ERS boarding station, running day and seat availability in IRCTC/NTES.

21. When should I reach Siliguri?

+

Preferably the evening before Day 0. A same-day arrival is workable only if it meets the organiser’s confirmed pickup cutoff with a realistic delay buffer.

22. Can I book a flight on Day 6?

+

This is not recommended. A full morning’s walking followed by a long drive makes a reliable airport arrival difficult. Stay in Siliguri and fly the next day.

23. Is the final Siliguri hotel included?

+

Not in the proposed six-night trek plan. Ask for a separate hotel and transfer arrangement if you want BOOTpaths to coordinate it.

24. Are airport and station pickups included?

+

Only if the final quotation specifically says so. A Siliguri group pickup does not automatically include separate transfers from IXB and NJP.

25. What if my flight or train is late?

+

Contact the organiser immediately. A delayed pickup or catch-up transfer depends on road access and staffing and may cost extra; catching the group cannot be guaranteed.

26. Does the trail go along the Nepal border?

+

Yes, parts of the traditional route and some accommodation lie along or across the border. The guide must confirm the permitted route and overnight arrangements for every participant.

27. What identification should an Indian participant carry?

+

Carry original government-issued photo ID and copies. Because Nepal-side access may be involved, confirm the accepted documents for this exact itinerary; do not assume Aadhaar alone is sufficient.

28. Can foreign nationals join?

+

Only after the operator verifies nationality-specific park, border, visa, accommodation and route restrictions. A route suitable for Indian nationals cannot automatically be promised to everyone.

29. Are permits included?

+

The source offer includes permits and local taxes. Confirm which permits, nationality categories and any additional fees are covered in the revised quote.

30. Can I add Darjeeling before or after the trek?

+

Yes, as a separate travel arrangement. Allow sufficient time and clarify luggage, hotel and transfer costs; it is not part of the stated route.

Comfort and Equipment (Q31 – Q45) 31 – 45

31. Will we stay in tents every night?

+

No. The intended style is simple mountain accommodation. The vendor’s tent alternative requires advance confirmation of equipment, permits, facilities and suitable conditions.

32. Will I have a private room?

+

Not necessarily. Sharing and dormitory arrangements depend on the properties and group size. Request your preference early; single rooms may be unavailable.

33. Are toilets attached to the rooms?

+

Do not assume so. Shared toilets are common in basic trekking accommodation. Confirm property-specific arrangements and any accessibility concerns before booking.

34. Will there be hot showers and heating?

+

These are not guaranteed. Hot water may be limited or paid, and rooms may be unheated. Bring adequate warm layers and follow the host’s heating-safety instructions.

35. What food will be served?

+

Expect simple local meals suited to trekking. The exact menu depends on supplies and kitchens. Long walking days may require packed lunches.

36. Can vegetarian or special diets be arranged?

+

Vegetarian meals are generally practical, but all requirements need notice. Strict allergy, vegan or gluten-free needs require direct confirmation of ingredients and kitchen limitations.

37. Is drinking water provided?

+

The final package must specify treated-water provision and charges. Carry reusable bottles and ask about refills before each day; untreated streams are not a safe default.

38. Will I get mobile reception and Wi-Fi?

+

Coverage is patchy and Wi-Fi cannot be relied on. Near the border, watch for unintended roaming. Give family a realistic communication plan before leaving.

39. Can I charge my phone or camera?

+

Some properties offer limited charging, sometimes at extra cost. Bring a power bank and protect batteries from cold; do not rely on a socket every night.

40. Can I pay digitally on the trail?

+

Sometimes, but network and electricity failures can interrupt payment. Carry enough small-denomination cash for agreed extras and keep it secure.

41. Can I offload my backpack?

+

Only if porter or animal support is arranged. Confirm availability, charges, weight limits, route coverage and suitable working conditions. Carry all daytime essentials yourself.

42. What shoes should I wear?

+

Well-fitting, broken-in trekking shoes with reliable grip. Test them on descents with your trekking socks. Bring more weather-resistant footwear if winter conditions require it.

43. Do I need a sleeping bag?

+

That depends on the confirmed bedding and accommodation plan. Ask before packing or renting, especially for Phalut and winter departures.

44. Are trekking poles useful?

+

Yes, especially on the long descent and uneven ground. Practise using them beforehand and stow them safely in crowded rooms and vehicles.

45. What should I carry for menstrual care?

+

Bring the products you normally use, spare underwear, sanitiser and sealable disposal bags. Water and privacy can be limited; carry used products out for proper disposal.

Health and Booking (Q46 – Q59) 46 – 59

46. Will we definitely see snow?

+

No. Snow depends on recent weather, season and elevation. Winter may bring bare, icy ground as well as snowfall; either can affect the route.

47. When is the park closed?

+

The usual monsoon closure is 16 June–15 September. The operator must check current park orders, temporary restrictions and the opening status for your exact dates.

48. Will we see a red panda?

+

Possibly, but sightings are rare and never guaranteed. Keep quiet, remain on the trail and follow the guide’s wildlife- distance instructions.

49. How fit should I be?

+

Prepare for repeated long walks with hills and your planned pack. Use the eight-week plan and discuss difficulty completing practice outings with the organiser.

50. Can I get altitude sickness even if I am fit?

+

Yes. Fitness is not protection against altitude illness. Follow the reporting and ascent guidance in the health section. [8]

51. What if I have headache or nausea?

+

Tell the guide immediately and do not continue ascending while symptomatic. Worsening symptoms or serious warning signs require descent and medical help.

52. Should I take Diamox preventively?

+

Discuss acetazolamide with a qualified clinician before travel. Do not use another trekker’s prescription or treat medication as a substitute for an appropriate ascent plan.

53. Can I join with a medical condition?

+

Disclose relevant conditions, allergies and regular medicines. Seek individual clinical advice and confirm that the trek’s remoteness and support arrangements are appropriate for you.

54. Is a doctor or oxygen cylinder included?

+

The vendor lists a guide’s first-aid kit only. Doctor support, oxygen, trained staffing and emergency equipment must be separately confirmed before they are advertised.

55. How does evacuation work?

+

The guide coordinates the safest available route and transport. Weather, communication and road access can delay help; there is no assumed helicopter or immediate vehicle rescue.

56. Do I need insurance?

+

Trek insurance is included in this cost. BOOTpaths gives safety the first priority and hence the insurance made compulsory.

57. What happens in severe weather?

+

The route, timings or overnight stops may change, or the trek may end early. Participant safety and legal access take priority over completing the advertised route.

58. Can I use a drone or play music?

+

Do not assume drone permission in this protected border landscape. Avoid loud music; keep the experience quiet for wildlife, residents and fellow trekkers.

59. What are the cancellation and early-exit rules?

+

See BOOTpaths’ policy covering cancellation, replacement, weather changes, missed transfers, unused services and extra costs before payment.
