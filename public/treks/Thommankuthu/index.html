About the Trek

Thommankuthu
Riverbed Trek

The walk begins at Thommankuthu, about 18 km from Thodupuzha. The route takes in Thommankuthu's seven-step cascade and its rock pools, then Ezhunilakuthu, the other named waterfall in the visitor area.

From there, the guided route follows the Kannadiyar upstream into denser forest. Expect uneven rock underfoot and damp stretches near the water. The deeper forest section leads to Nakkayam and Chekuthankuthu, two larger waterfalls on the permitted trekking route.

Kerala Tourism also lists Nakkayam Cave, Chekuthan Kuthu Cave, and Plapothu Cave along the waterfall corridor. The planned trek covers about 5 km into the forest and 5 km back, with the day's turnaround point subject to forest clearance and trail conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about the Thommankuthu Riverbed Trek

Is this trek suitable for beginners?

+

Yes, if you are comfortable walking on uneven ground. The distance is moderate (10 km round trip), but sections involve wet rocks and riverbed walking.

Can we take a dip in the waterfall?

+

Yes, you can take a dip at designated safe spots near the waterfalls, such as Chekuthankuthu, under the guidance of our trek leaders and local forest guides. Always follow guide instructions regarding water depth and current.

Will we reach Chekuthankuthu on every departure?

+

We plan to reach Chekuthankuthu on every departure. However, the exact turnaround point depends on forest department permissions, weather, and river water levels on the day.

Can we enter the caves along the trail?

+

The caves (Nakkayam, Chekuthan Kuthu, and Plapothu) are viewed from the trail. Entering them is subject to forest department rules and guide assessment on the day.

Do I need to book a forest permit separately?

+

No. Forest entry and trekking permits are arranged by BOOTpaths as part of your booking.

What should I carry for this trail?

+

Wear shoes with good grip on wet rock. Bring a change of clothes (especially if taking a dip), a towel, a reusable water bottle, rainwear (during monsoon/pre-monsoon), and personal medications.

Which month should I choose?

+

November to May is the best season for riverbed trekking when water levels are manageable and trails are clearer. Post-monsoon (Nov–Jan) offers lush greenery and full waterfalls; February to May offers easier riverbed walking.
