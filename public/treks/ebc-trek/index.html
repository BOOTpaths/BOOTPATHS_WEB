About the Trek

The World's Most
Iconic Trek

The Everest Base Camp Trek is not just a trek — it is a pilgrimage into the heart of the Himalayas, through ancient Sherpa culture, rhododendron forests, and glacial valleys that lead to the foot of the world's highest mountain.

Beginning with a thrilling flight into Lukla's high-altitude airstrip, you'll journey through the legendary Khumbu Valley — passing through bustling Namche Bazaar, the sacred Tengboche Monastery, and pristine alpine villages — before finally standing at Everest Base Camp at 5,364 metres above sea level.

The bonus summit of Kala Patthar (5,545m) offers the most iconic views of Mount Everest — a moment that will stay with you for a lifetime. With Bootpaths as your guide, every step is managed with expertise, care, and passion for the mountains.

In the evening, attend a comprehensive pre-trek briefing with your lead guide, review medical and gear checklists, and enjoy a traditional Nepali welcome dinner with your team.

Board an early morning twin-otter flight to Tenzing-Hillary Airport in Lukla — one of the world's most famous mountain runways. After meeting our porter and Sherpa crew, the trek officially begins! We descend through pine forests along the turquoise Dudh Koshi river, passing carved Buddhist mani stones and lush terraced fields to the riverside settlement of Phakding.

Follow the trail crossing multiple suspension bridges decorated with fluttering prayer flags. Pass through Monjo to enter Sagarmatha National Park. On clear days, catch your very first view of Mount Everest peaking over the Lhotse-Nuptse ridge!

Acclimatisation Day in Namche Bazaar (3,440 m)

Acclimatisation is the golden key to Everest Base Camp success. Today we take a scenic hike up to the legendary Everest View Hotel (3,880 m). Sit on the terrace with warm tea while taking in the majestic amphitheater of Everest, Ama Dablam, and Thamserku. Visit the Sherpa Museum and Khumjung village before descending back to Namche to explore local artisan shops and warm German bakeries.

A breathtaking day of trekking along high valley contours with constant views of Everest, Nuptse, and Ama Dablam. Descend to the river at Phunki Thenga for lunch, then climb through fragrant rhododendron and birch woods to Tengboche ridge. Visit the world-renowned Tengboche Monastery — the spiritual heart of the Khumbu — with the iconic needle peak of Ama Dablam framed directly behind.

Descend gently through Deboche and cross the rushing Imja Khola river on a steel bridge. Pass the ancient village of Pangboche and hike past hundreds of stone mani walls. As you climb above the tree line into the alpine zone, the landscape transforms into wide, windswept pastures lined with stone walls. Reach Dingboche, known as the 'Summer Valley', with views of Lhotse and Island Peak.

From the prayer-flag-strewn summit, enjoy an awe-inspiring 360° panorama including Makalu (the world's 5th highest mountain), Island Peak, and the massive south face of Lhotse.

Trail Highlights: Thugla Pass mountaineers memorial chortens, Khumbu Glacier lateral moraine, Pumori and Nuptse views.

Trek across the high alpine plateau to Dughla (Thugla), then ascend the steep terminal moraine of the Khumbu Glacier to Thugla Pass (4,830 m). Here stand poignant stone memorials dedicated to climbers who lost their lives on Everest. The trail then follows the glacier's lateral moraine beneath the soaring pyramid of Nuptse and Pumori to the rugged outpost of Lobuche.

The most anticipated day of the journey! We trek across rugged glacial moraines to Gorak Shep, the highest permanent settlement on the route. After a warm lunch and dropping heavy packs, we continue the final 3-hour trek over the Khumbu Glacier to Everest Base Camp (5,364 m). Stand proudly at the foot of the world's highest peak, surrounded by prayer flags and the thundering Khumbu Icefall. You made it!

As you reach the prayer-flagged summit, the morning sun bursts over Mount Everest, illuminating its sheer south face and peak in dazzling gold. This is the most famous Everest photograph in the world. After descending to Gorak Shep for breakfast, we continue downward to Pheriche for a warm meal and restful sleep in thicker air.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

No prior trekking experience is required, but you should be in good physical fitness. The trek involves walking 5–8 hours per day over 14 days. We recommend training with regular hiking, cardio, and endurance exercises at least 2–3 months before the trek.

The best seasons are pre-monsoon (March–May) and post-monsoon (September–November). Spring offers rhododendron blooms and clearer skies; autumn has the most stable weather. December–February is possible but very cold. Monsoon (June–August) brings heavy rain and trail hazards.

Altitude sickness (AMS) is a real concern above 3,500m. Our itinerary includes built-in acclimatisation days at Namche Bazaar and Dingboche. Our guides monitor symptoms daily using pulse oximeters. In serious cases, immediate descent or helicopter evacuation is arranged. Travel insurance covering high-altitude evacuation is mandatory.

Accommodation is in teahouses (locally owned guesthouses). Rooms are basic but comfortable with twin beds. Private rooms are available at lower altitudes; higher up, shared dorms may be necessary. Hot showers and charging facilities are available for a small fee.

You need: (1) Nepal Tourist Visa — available on arrival at Kathmandu Airport; (2) TIMS Card; (3) Sagarmatha National Park Permit. Bootpaths handles all trekking permits on your behalf as part of the package.

Yes, travel insurance is absolutely mandatory for all Bootpaths treks. Your policy must cover: medical emergencies, high-altitude trekking above 5,000m, helicopter evacuation, and trip cancellation. We will require proof of insurance before the trek begins.

Our standard group size is 4–12 trekkers, ensuring a personal experience. Private treks (solo or small groups) can be arranged at a premium. Each group has a dedicated lead guide plus assistant guides/porters based on group size.

Key items: layered clothing (thermal base layers, fleece, down jacket, waterproof shell), sturdy trekking boots (broken in), trekking poles, sleeping bag (rated to -15°C), sun protection (SPF50+, sunglasses, lip balm), and water purification. A detailed packing list is shared after booking.
