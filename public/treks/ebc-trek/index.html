About the Trail

Everest Base Camp
14-Day Expedition

The sound of the Dudh Koshi follows you through the pine forests. Above the river, long suspension bridges connect the villages of the Khumbu. By the time you reach Namche Bazaar, the peaks you've seen in photographs begin to take shape above the rooftops.

Then comes Ama Dablam, rising above the valley as you walk towards Tengboche. Higher up, the trees give way to open valleys, stone-walled villages and glacial moraine. You'll pause at the climbers' memorials above Thukla, spend a night at Gorak Shep, and follow the rocky trail beside the Khumbu Glacier to Everest Base Camp at 5,364 m.

The next morning, weather permitting, you'll set out before dawn for Kala Patthar and watch the first light reach Everest, Nuptse and the surrounding peaks. There's a quieter journey running through these fourteen days, too. You learn to slow down on a steep climb, settle into the rhythm of walking, and look forward to a hot meal with the people who shared the day with you.

Why This Trek

Why Trek to Everest Base Camp?

Key moments and perspectives along the classic trail to the foot of Mount Everest

01

See the great peaks of the Khumbu

Look for Ama Dablam around Tengboche and Pangboche, Pumori as you approach the upper Khumbu, and Everest, Lhotse and Nuptse along the route.

02

Cross the suspension bridges of the Dudh Koshi

Hear the river below your boots from the Hillary Suspension Bridge, then take on the steep forest climb to Namche Bazaar.

03

Spend time in Sherpa villages

Explore the shops and lanes of Namche, visit the Sherpa Museum, and experience everyday life in family-run teahouses.

04

Visit Tengboche Monastery

Walk around the monastery before descending to Deboche, with Ama Dablam and other Himalayan peaks framing the valley on clear days.

05

Pause at the memorials above Thukla

Stop at the stone memorials and prayer flags honouring the Sherpa mountaineers and climbers who lost their lives on Everest and neighbouring peaks.

06

Walk beside the Khumbu Glacier

Move through a stark landscape of loose rock and glacial moraine from Gorak Shep to Everest Base Camp.

07

Watch the sunrise from Kala Patthar

Start in the dark and see Everest and its neighbours emerge in the morning light from the highest point on our itinerary (5,650 m).

08

Make room for an inward journey

Long stretches on foot give you time to think; shared climbs and quiet mornings become the stories you bring home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about preparing, walking, and succeeding on Everest Base Camp

It's a difficult multi-day trek. Expect steep climbs, uneven terrain and several long days of walking. The return from Pheriche to Namche and from Namche to Lukla each takes around nine hours on the planned itinerary.

Speak with BOOTpaths about your recent hiking experience. This itinerary suits trekkers who can already manage long hill walks and repeat them on consecutive days. If most of your experience is on short, easy trails, build up with longer treks before EBC.

Start two to three months ahead if you can. Build towards longer uphill walks, train for descents as well as climbs, and practise hiking on consecutive days in the boots and daypack you'll use in Nepal. Stair climbing and leg-strength work can complement time on the trail.

The planned route is a walking trek. You should be comfortable on loose rock, steep steps, uneven descents and suspension bridges.

There are two dedicated acclimatisation days, one in Namche Bazaar and one in Dingboche. Both include a hike to a higher elevation and a return to the same teahouse for the night.

Tell your guide promptly, especially if you develop a new headache, nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue after ascending. Avoid going higher while you have symptoms. Worsening symptoms require prompt assessment and may require descent.

It's a planned part of the trek. The guide will assess weather, trail conditions and how trekkers are feeling before the ascent.

Base Camp is where you experience the glacier landscape and stand at the foot of the expedition route. On clear days, Kala Patthar offers the better view of Everest itself.

You'll spend the first and last scheduled nights in a Kathmandu hotel. On the trail, you'll sleep in mountain teahouses with basic rooms and communal dining spaces.

Facilities vary by teahouse and may be shared, especially higher up. Hot showers and hot water may be available for an additional charge.

Breakfast, lunch and dinner are included during the trek, along with tea or coffee two to three times a day as scheduled. The Kathmandu hotel breakfasts are included. Other meals in Kathmandu are at your own expense.

Menus vary by lodge. Common options include dal bhat, rice and noodle dishes, soups and simple breakfasts. Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements before booking so we can check what the lodges can accommodate.

Bring refillable bottles. Ask your guide about refill and treatment options at each stop. Bottled water and hot water are personal expenses.

Wi-Fi and charging are mostly chargeable for a fee by the teahouse after Namche Bazaar. Availability varies, so carry a power bank and download anything you'll need offline before leaving Kathmandu.

Bring broken-in trekking boots, warm layers, a waterproof outer shell, gloves, a warm hat, sunglasses and a headlamp. Pack for cold mornings and nights, especially near Gorak Shep and for the pre-dawn Kala Patthar hike.

Carry Nepalese rupees for personal purchases such as snacks, charging, showers and Wi-Fi. Arrange your trail cash in Kathmandu before heading out.

The trek includes a porter allowance of up to 11 kg per person for your main trekking bag. Confirm domestic flight baggage rules separately before packing.

Keep water, snacks, a warm layer, rain protection, sun protection, personal medication and valuables with you. Pack anything you'll want access to while walking.
